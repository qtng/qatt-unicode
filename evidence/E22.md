## Notes

Online presences of the script, as listed by the proposer on 2026-10-10. None carries a date of first publication except the Omniglot page, whose modification date is shown.

### Findings

- **qatt.org:** website of the proposer. Not independent evidence.
- **Omniglot, https://www.omniglot.com/writing/quocamtantu.htm:** page on the script by Simon Ager (© 1998–2026), last modified 19.08.26 as shown on the page. It describes a phonetic script for Vietnamese with 22 stem characters and 110 branch characters, built from four basic strokes, with tones marked by lines or circles; it says the script "was not widely used" and dates one of the two manuscripts to the reign of Emperor Thiệu Trị. It does not mention Unicode. The sample text and translation are credited to the Vietnamese Wikipedia. It links to English and Vietnamese Wikipedia, a Facebook group, a scan on archive.org and the web editor below. It therefore depends on the Vietnamese Wikipedia and is not independent of it.
- **Web converter, https://quocamtantu.vercel.app/:** page titled "Bộ chuyển đổi Quốc Ngữ -> Quốc Âm Tân Tự (QATT)", a converter from Quốc Ngữ to the script, by an unknown author, found on social media. The page text shows no author, date or sources. How the converted output is rendered (images, private-use characters or a font) was not established.
- **YouTube video, https://youtu.be/1XPtftoxcjE:** "Maruhi Academy: Quốc Âm Tân Tự Script", channel Maruhi Academy・丸秘学園 (title and channel from YouTube's oEmbed data). About 12,000 views on 2026-10-10, as reported by the proposer; the view count, upload date, language and sources of the video were not checked here.
- **Facebook group "qatt":** reported by the proposer; not checked here. URL, creation date and number of members to be added.

### Open

- How the converter and the group write the script (the written forms in online discussion mentioned in the claim); relevant for the need for encoding.
